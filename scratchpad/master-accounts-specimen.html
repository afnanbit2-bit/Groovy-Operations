What each screen is for

Overviewma-overview

Cash by holder — the drawer, Afnan, Ammar, MCB, the till, floats, the TCS account — this month in and out, owed to us and by us, runway; the three concerns of the day in plain words, the 30-day cash calendar and the spend map.

Couriers & collectionsma-couriers

The first mile of money in: PostEx CPRs derived from the parcels, issued Tuesday and Friday, collected in cash by a named holder with the receipt attached; TCS credited at 90 days; Blue-Ex's legacy balance; Bykea via Raees.

Parties & the vendor pagema-parties · ma-party

Every vendor, customer, courier, employee, owner and bank: what a vendor provides, terms with history, the rate card with history, open orders, the credit/debit ledger with a running balance, statement, aging.

Purchasingma-purchasing

Purchase orders assigned to production POs, receipts from fabric-in and vendor returns, bills matched three ways, and the queue of documents the validation engine flagged.

Paymentsma-payments

The pay-day list from every vendor's terms, pay runs, allocation of each payment to bills, advances and retention releases.

Costingma-costing

A cost sheet per production PO: fabric at receipt rate, stitching, washing, embellishment and trims per piece; cost per piece by article; margin by channel; a missing leg named, never zero.

Ledgerma-ledger

Every posting from every source on one line shape, filterable by date, source and account, each line opening the record it came from.

Profit & lossma-pnl

Revenue by channel, cost of goods, operating expenses by category, month against month and against budget.

Balance sheetma-balance

What the company holds and owes on a day: cash, banks, receivables, stock at cost, payables, loans, capital.

Cash flowma-cashflow

Where cash came from and went, by kind, with the next 30, 60 and 90 days projected from what is already known to be due.

Sales & couriersma-sales

Invoices by channel, collections, courier remittances matched to their receipts, pay-later reminders.

Owed & owingma-aging

Receivables and payables aged into buckets, per customer, courier and vendor, with the pay-day and collection calendar.

Moneyma-money

One page per holder: statement, running balance, transfers pending the receiver's confirmation, reconciliation against the bank's or the person's own count.

Savingsma-savings · -targets · -recurring · -networth

The owners' own book: where the Payfast money sits, spend by category, subscriptions per owner with the annual cost, targets like the marriage and the car with the monthly need and an ETA, assets and liabilities, net worth, reconciliation.

Loanma-loan

The ₨15 lac borrowed from savings: draws and repayments as one document on both books, the outstanding on both overviews, the repayment pace, the Payfast payout rule.

Taxma-tax

Every document carries a tax block (sales, services, withholding, or none — chosen, never absent); the quarter's output tax, input tax and withholding by vendor, for the accountant.

Budgetsma-budgets

A monthly budget per category, suggested from the last six months and accepted or overruled by an owner; variance tracked.

Insightsma-insights

What the app has learned: baselines, anomalies, forecasts, rate drift and a data-quality score, each with its basis stated.

Close & auditma-close

Quarter-end (fiscal year from 1 July): reconcile every holder, lock the quarter, export the books; a soft month close where a month matters; the audit trail of every journal and override.

Settingsma-settings

Both charts of accounts, holders, pay days and CPR days, tolerances, tax defaults, the payout rule, fiscal year, go-live.

How the charts are drawn

  • HTML and CSS, the way the Marketing reports draw theirs: bars are boxes, a donut is a conic gradient, a line is an SVG path with every label in HTML. No chart library.
  • Series colours are new tokens, validated for colour-blind separation on the app's light and dark surfaces. Text never wears a series colour; identity comes from the swatch beside it.
  • Every chart has a table twin, one filter row scopes the whole screen, and hovering any mark shows its value.
  • A donut only for a part-to-whole glance with six segments or fewer; rankings and comparisons are bars.

How the money speaks

  • Every line of spend — a category, a vendor, a courier's fees, salaries, a subscription — carries a state: fine, watch or concern, from three tests: its own six-month history, its budget, and its terms and commitments. The worst wins.
  • Each state is a sentence with the number and the comparison, its basis, and one action. "This is the drop" is remembered and teaches the baseline.
  • The spend map is sized by rupees and tinted by state; the cash calendar puts Tuesday and Friday CPRs against Wednesday and Saturday pay days and names a day the holders cannot fund.

How the app learns

  • Nothing is guessed. Every learned number carries its basis: the months it was computed over, the count, the method.
  • Baselines first (medians, seasonality), then anomalies (a figure far outside its own history), then forecasts (cash runway from what is already known to be due), then budget suggestions.
  • Learning is only as good as the entry discipline, so the data-quality score is on the same screen.
  • All of it is computed from rollups the app already writes; no third party sees the books.
